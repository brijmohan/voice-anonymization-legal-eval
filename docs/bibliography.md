# The reference library

Every work this body of research rests on, in one place, categorised by
the question it answers rather than by topic. The machine-readable form
is [`references.bib`](references.bib), where each entry carries its place
in this taxonomy in its `keywords` field. Both files are generated by
`scripts/build_bibliography.py`.

**262 entries.** 24 were added by hand while building
this library, because they were missing from every bibliography we had.
That absence is worth stating plainly: the thesis bibliography stops at
2021 and the current paper's bibliography is about content privacy, so
between them they omitted Opinion 05/2014 and Cohen and Nissim, the two
documents this framework is built on. A library assembled only from what
we had already cited would have had a hole exactly where the foundations
are.

## How to read it

**The working set** is what this project actually depends on, and each
entry there says what the work establishes and where we use it.
Everything else is categorised background: real references, placed, but
without a note each. Distinguishing the two is the point, because a
library where every entry looks equally load bearing tells a reader
nothing about what to read first.

Metadata added by hand was checked against the publisher or the preprint
server. A few entries carry a note saying a field is still to be
confirmed, which is deliberate: an unverified author initial is recorded
as unverified rather than guessed.

Deliberately excluded: work on low-resource ASR, code-switching and
pronunciation modelling, which shares a bibliography with this research
but not a subject. One exception is kept, a 2025 paper on anonymizing
code-switching speech, because there the two subjects meet.

### A. The legal frame

**Party (2014).** Opinion 05/2014 on Anonymisation Techniques. European Commission.

> The three conjunctive criteria the whole framework implements. Singling out, linkability and inference, and inference means attribute values rather than membership.

`art29wp2014opinion05`

**Tomashenko et al. (2020).** Introducing the VoicePrivacy initiative. Interspeech.

> The origin of the attacker taxonomy we inherit, ignorant, lazy-informed and semi-informed, and of the enrollment and trial split.

`tomashenko20interspeech`

**ISO/IEC (2022).** ISO/IEC 27559:2022 Information Security, Cybersecurity and Privacy Protection: Privacy Enhancing Data De-identification Framework. International Organization for Standardization.

> The existing de-identification standard. Any proposal of ours has to say how it relates to this.

`iso27559`

**ISO/IEC (2022).** ISO/IEC 24745 Information Security, Cybersecurity and Privacy Protection: Biometric Information Protection. International Organization for Standardization.

> The standards family ZEBRA was already offered to, which is the route a voice anonymization metric would travel.

`iso24745`

**Tomashenko et al. (2024).** The VoicePrivacy 2024 Challenge Evaluation Plan. arXiv:2404.02677.

> The evaluation plan our dataset decisions are inherited from.

`tomashenko2024voiceprivacy2024`

**Board (2024).** Opinion 28/2024 on Certain Data Protection Aspects Related to the Processing of Personal Data in the Context of AI Models. European Data Protection Board.

> A regulator's anonymity test for models rather than datasets: extraction probability negligible, query risk insignificant. The closest thing to a threshold the field has been given.

`edpb2024opinion28`

**Cohen and Nissim (2020).** Towards Formalizing the GDPR's Notion of Singling Out. Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences. arXiv:1904.06009

> Predicate singling out, the formal basis of our Singling Out metric. Also proves differential privacy implies PSO security while k-anonymity does not, which is why the k-anonymity literature in section H is background rather than a solution.

`cohen2020singlingout`

**Vauquier et al. (2025).** Legally Validated Evaluation Framework for Voice Anonymization. Interspeech 2025.

> This project's own paper. Singling Out and Linkability, and the finding that the EER is close to blind to residual re-identification risk.

`vauquier25interspeech`

**Anon. (2025).** Unifying Re-Identification, Attribute Inference, and Data Reconstruction Risks in Differential Privacy. arXiv preprint. arXiv:2507.06969

> Puts re-identification, attribute inference and reconstruction on one footing under differential privacy. Directly relevant to reporting our three criteria together.

`khalili2025unifying`

## Gaps worth filling

Stated so they are not mistaken for coverage.

- **Section B holds 11 entries against section A's 26 and section D's 68**, which is
  odd for the part of the field this project is actually about. The
  reason is real rather than an artefact of this library: proposals to
  replace the equal error rate are almost all from 2024 onward, and four
  of them arrived in one year.
- **The pseudo-speaker subsection holds 0 entries**, which is obligation G3.
  `legal_eval.pseudo_speaker` implements a measure of it, so either we
  are ahead of the literature or we have not found it.
- **The attacker-model subsection holds 2 entries.** For a criterion whose
  regulatory obligation is to state the attacker concretely, that is a
  thin literature to lean on.
- **ISO/IEC 24745 is referenced but not read.** It is the standards
  family a voice anonymization metric would actually travel to, and the
  entry here is a placeholder for the real thing.
